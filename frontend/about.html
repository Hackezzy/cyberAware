About CyberAware

What this is

CyberAware teaches people to recognize and respond to common cyber attacks by actually doing it, not by reading about it. Every simulation walks through a real attack pattern step by step; every tool analyzes something you give it and explains exactly what it found; every quiz scores a real judgment call, with an explanation either way. The goal is to leave you able to spot these attacks in real life — and, for anyone considering a cybersecurity role, to build the kind of practical judgment an entry-level position actually expects, not just facts to recite.

Why it's built this way

The topics covered here aren't arbitrary. They're grounded in published breach data and established security frameworks — including the Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report, the MITRE ATT&CK framework, the OWASP Top 10, and the domains covered by the CompTIA Security+ certification — so the difficulty and priority of each topic reflects how often it actually happens and how much it actually matters, not a guess. Content is organized into five domains (Email & Message Security, Human & Social Engineering, Network & Wireless Security, Malware & Ransomware, and Identity & Access) across five difficulty levels, from single obvious red flags up to ambiguous, realistic scenarios. The Roadmap lays this whole structure out and tracks what you've completed in each one.

What data is collected

For almost the entire site, the honest answer is none — no backend server, no accounts, no sign-ups. The one deliberate exception is the real-time threat check on the URL Safety Checker tool, which sends the URL text (and only the URL text) to a small backend server so it can be checked against Google Safe Browsing's live threat database — a real check that a purely offline tool structurally cannot do. That page explains this in full at the point it happens.

  • Every other tool — the Password Strength Analyzer, the Credential Reuse Check, the Phishing Email Scanner — analyzes what you type or paste entirely inside your own browser. None of it is ever sent anywhere, logged, or stored outside your device.
  • Your progress (which simulations, tools, and quizzes you've completed, and your quiz scores) is saved only in your browser's local storage, so it survives a page reload but never leaves your device. You can review it any time on the My Progress page, and clear it whenever you want.
  • There is no analytics, no tracking script, and no third-party embed of any kind, on any page.

Educational use only

No real attacks are ever performed by this site. Every simulated brand, company, and person (like "SecurePay" or "Meridian Bookkeeping") is fictional. When a tool asks you to try a practice password or a fake account label, don't type something you actually use elsewhere — use something similar in structure instead.
