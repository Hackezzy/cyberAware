Ransomware Simulation

Step through how ransomware actually spreads through a small business, from a single opened attachment to every file being locked. Nothing here is real — "Meridian Bookkeeping" is a made-up small business built just for this walkthrough.

Step 1 of 6

1. The Delivery

A bookkeeper at Meridian Bookkeeping opens an email attachment that looks like a routine invoice.

Downloads

📄 Invoice_40218.pdf.exe

What's happening

  • • The real file extension is .exe (a program), not .pdf — the ".pdf" is just part of the filename, designed to look harmless at a glance.
  • • Windows hides known file extensions by default, so many people would only see "Invoice_40218.pdf" and never notice the .exe at all.

2. The Foothold

Double-clicking the file runs it. Nothing visibly happens — that's by design.

[background] disabling File History...

[background] deleting Volume Shadow Copies...

[background] scanning for network shares...

[background] establishing outbound connection...

What's happening

  • • The malware quietly disables Windows' built-in backup and file-recovery features first, before doing anything else — so there's nothing easy to restore from later.
  • • It scans for other computers and shared drives on the same network it can reach, to spread beyond just this one machine.

3. The Spread

Over the next few hours, files are quietly encrypted — on this computer and every shared drive it could reach.

C:\Users\bookkeeper\Documents\Q3_Payroll.xlsx → Q3_Payroll.xlsx.locked

\\MERIDIAN-NAS\Shared\Client_Invoices\ → encrypting (212 files)

\\MERIDIAN-NAS\Shared\Tax_Records\ → encrypting (89 files)

What's happening

  • • Encryption is deliberately slow and quiet so it isn't noticed until it's mostly finished — by the time anyone sees a problem, the damage is already done.
  • • A shared network drive means one infected laptop can lock every other computer's files too, not just its own.

4. The Lock

A note appears. Every file on the network is now unreadable.

YOUR FILES HAVE BEEN ENCRYPTED

To recover your files, send 1.4 BTC to the address below within 72 hours. The price doubles after the deadline. Do not contact law enforcement.

bc1q_x7f...9k2m (payment address)

What's happening

  • • The countdown and "do not contact law enforcement" warning are both pressure tactics, the same urgency trick used in phishing, just with much higher stakes.
  • • Cryptocurrency payment is untraceable and irreversible — there's no bank to call and dispute the charge afterward.

5. The Choice

Paying feels like the fastest fix. It usually isn't.

If you pay

  • • No guarantee the attacker actually sends a working decryption key.
  • • Confirms this business pays, inviting repeat attacks.
  • • Funds further attacks against other victims.

What to do instead

  • • Disconnect the machine from the network immediately, to stop the spread.
  • • Restore from an offline backup that was never connected when the attack happened.
  • • Report the incident — law enforcement and cybersecurity agencies track ransom payment addresses and can sometimes help.

What's happening

This decision only has a good outcome if it was prepared for before the attack — specifically, by keeping backups that ransomware on the network can't also reach and encrypt.

6. The Aftermath

Ransomware hits small businesses hardest — here's how to actually prevent this scene from happening.

Ransomware is involved in the large majority of breaches at small and medium businesses, far more than at large organizations — attackers assume smaller businesses have weaker backups and smaller security budgets.

Prevention checklist

  • • Keep at least one backup that's offline or otherwise disconnected from the network — a backup ransomware can also encrypt isn't a real backup.
  • • Never enable macros or run an attachment you weren't expecting, even from a sender you recognize.
  • • Show file extensions in your file browser instead of hiding them, so a "PDF" that's secretly a program is easier to spot.
  • • Keep software patched — many ransomware infections spread through known, already-fixed vulnerabilities.
