Smishing Simulation

Step through how SMS phishing works — the text-message version of the scam. Nothing here is real; "SwiftParcel" is a made-up delivery brand built just for this walkthrough.

Step 1 of 6

1. The Text

An attacker sends a text pretending to be a delivery company, from a number that isn't SwiftParcel's real one.

New message (attacker's view)

From: +1 (302) 555-0148

"SwiftParcel: your package is on hold. Pay a small redelivery fee here: bit.ly/xk29p"

Red flags at this stage

  • • Real delivery companies text from a small set of known short codes, not a random personal-looking phone number.
  • • A shortened link hides the real destination — there's no way to tell where it goes just by reading it.

2. It Arrives

The text lands in a messages app next to ordinary, real texts.

M

Mom

Don't forget dinner Sunday!

2:14 PM
SP

+1 (302) 555-0148

SwiftParcel: your package is on hold...

2:31 PM
DP

Dr. Patel's Office

Reminder: appointment tomorrow at 10am

3:02 PM

Red flags at this stage

  • • Text messages carry an unearned sense of trust — people are more used to scrutinizing email than texts.
  • • It sits comfortably among genuinely expected texts, which makes it easy to read without much suspicion.

3. The Hook

A held package and a "small fee" combine curiosity with just enough urgency to prompt a tap.

"SwiftParcel: your package is on hold at our facility due to an unpaid customs fee of $2.49. Confirm payment within 24 hours to avoid your item being returned to sender: bit.ly/xk29p"

Red flags at this stage

  • • A tiny fee ($2.49) is deliberately unalarming — small enough that entering a card feels low-risk, even though the real goal is capturing full card details, not the $2.49 itself.
  • • The 24-hour deadline nudges toward acting immediately, before stopping to check whether a package was even expected.

4. The Trap

Tapping the link opens a mobile page that looks like a delivery company's payment form.

swiftparcel-redeliver.top

SwiftParcel Redelivery

Pay $2.49 to release your package

Card number
Expiry & CVV
Billing ZIP code

Red flags at this stage

  • • The domain swiftparcel-redeliver.top isn't SwiftParcel's real site — a real courier's tracking domain doesn't change for a one-off payment page.
  • • The form asks for full card details plus the CVV and ZIP code — everything needed to use the card elsewhere, not just a $2.49 charge.

5. The Catch

The $2.49 goes through. The card details go somewhere else too.

[payment processed] $2.49 — SwiftParcel Redelivery

[attacker log] card details forwarded to card-testing service

What's happening

  • • A tiny successful charge also confirms the card is real and active — attackers often resell working card numbers, or use them for larger fraudulent purchases shortly after.

6. The Aftermath

What happens next, and how to actually avoid this one.

What happens next

  • • The card gets used or sold for larger fraudulent charges.
  • • The bank may flag and freeze the card, disrupting real purchases too.

What to do instead

  • • Don't tap links in unexpected delivery texts — open the courier's official app or website directly instead.
  • • Real delivery fees are essentially never collected by texting a link — treat that combination as an instant red flag.
  • • Check whether you're even expecting a package before doing anything else.
