Tailgating Simulation

Step through how someone can walk straight into a secure office without ever using a badge — just by relying on people's natural politeness. This is the simplest attack on the platform, and often the most overlooked.

Step 1 of 6

1. The Approach

Someone waits near a badge-locked office door, arms full of boxes.

📦📦

A person in a delivery vest, carrying two large boxes, standing just outside a badge-access door.

What's happening

A vest and boxes are enough to look like they belong there — nobody checks whether a delivery person is really from a delivery company.

2. The Courtesy

An employee badges in, sees someone struggling with boxes, and holds the door.

"Oh, here — let me get that for you."

What's happening

Holding a door for someone carrying things is basic politeness, not a security mistake anyone would think twice about — which is exactly why this works so reliably.

3. The Walk-In

The visitor walks straight in behind them — no badge, no sign-in, no check at all.

[badge reader] access granted — employee badge

[door] propped open — second entry not logged

What's happening

The badge system only recorded one valid entry — there's no record at all that a second, unauthorized person is now inside the building.

4. The Blend-In

Now inside, the visitor walks with purpose. Nobody questions them.

Carrying a clipboard and walking confidently past several desks, the visitor looks like they know exactly where they're going.

What's happening

People rarely challenge someone who looks like they belong, especially in an office too large for everyone to personally know every coworker by sight.

5. The Access

An unlocked, unattended workstation is sitting right there.

[workstation] session active — unlocked

[workstation] logged in as: bookkeeper@meridianbooks-corp.com

What's happening

Once inside the building, the physical break-in is already over — from here it's just a matter of finding one unlocked screen, one unattended desk, or a small device to quietly plug in.

6. The Aftermath

Digital security means little if the building itself is easy to walk into — here's how to actually prevent this.

Every other simulation on this site assumes the attacker is somewhere else on the internet. This one is a reminder that physical access to a device is often the fastest way past every other protection in place.

Prevention checklist

  • • It's okay to politely ask someone without a badge to use their own, or to direct them to reception — this isn't rude, it's normal.
  • • Lock your screen every time you step away from your desk, even for a minute.
  • • Report anyone in the building without a visible badge, especially near secure or sensitive areas.
  • • Businesses can use door alarms or badge-in-badge-out logging that flags when more people enter than badges were used.
